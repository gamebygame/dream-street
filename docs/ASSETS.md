# Assets, dependencies and sources

## Made by this project

Everything you see is generated by the project's JavaScript geometry code:
- the character bodies, five outfits, heads and hats, and shoes;
- the street, nine kinds of storefront, mannequins, trees, glass, props and bicycles.

There are no external character models, motion packs or textures. The eight staged props are record, pocket watch, cane, bouquet, rose, umbrella, puppet and cup; a scarf can also be generated. The 36 passers-by and two cyclists reuse one body template and a geometry cache, and each solves its own pose.

The dance is a set of continuous pose curves written for this project. The walk uses planted-foot constraints and a two-bone leg correction. Before they join the walker, passers-by browse windows, wait, chat, walk and cross the street on authored paths. Their joins are cubic Hermite curves that match their own speed at the start and the walker's at the end.

## References for motion (not copied)

- **The dance.** Jovi described the feeling of a Michael Jackson dance. We took from it the tip of the hat, weight shifts, toe and heel work, separation of the torso, and a release after each hold, and wrote whole phrases of our own. The entry point was the [official Smooth Criminal page](https://www.michaeljackson.com/watch/smooth-criminal/). No motion data was extracted from or copied out of the video, and we do not claim to reproduce the original choreography.
- **Props.** The [Stage Properties playlist at Jacob's Pillow](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/playlists/stage-properties/) describes how an object changes a dancer's relationships, space and focus. Our own short phrases grew from that idea: offering a bouquet with a turn of the wrist, a rose that leads the head, turning an umbrella in one hand, studying a watch, planting a cane. No images, recordings or video from the site are used.
- **Body language of the tender chapter.** Two sources: the [Royal Ballet School's Port de Bras class notes](https://ondemand.royalballetschool.org.uk/product/focus-class-port-de-bras/) on posture, weight, back, breath and gaze; and the distinctions in [Shafir et al.'s study of movement and emotion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4707271/) between timing, weight, sinking, gathering in and head posture. From them we wrote sustained weight shifts, gathering, studying, slow opening and pauses. We do not treat a research correlation as a formula for everyone's emotions, and we do not claim a ballet reproduction. No paid course content was used.
- **Arm swing.** The amplitudes follow [The elbow is the load-bearing joint during arm swing](https://www.cisejournal.org/journal/view.php?doi=10.5397%2Fcise.2023.00101) (Clinics in Shoulder and Elbow, 2023). Walking at 1.11 m/s, 15 healthy adults showed about 29.7° ± 10.2° of elbow flexion and 56.4° ± 12.7° of shoulder flexion. We use about 39° at the shoulder and 15°–45° at the elbow; these are design values, not a biomechanical simulation.
- **The costume.** The walker's old clothes follow Jovi's description of a down-at-heel English gentleman: long coat, top hat, tie and patches. No film stills, actor portraits or ready-made character assets were used.

## The original score

The music is an **original score**, synthesised live in the browser with the Web Audio API. It is a small rock band in the spirit of Michael Jackson's and Queen's records, played clean in the manner of 1970s rock, with no synthesisers, pads or drum-machine sounds:
- a dry kit: kick, snare, hi-hats, crash and toms, with a shaker in the ballad;
- electric bass;
- doubled rhythm guitars, a lead guitar and piano.

Two sounds are computed once while the page loads, in plain JavaScript, from fixed seeds:
- the rhythm guitar's power chords, from a plucked-string (Karplus–Strong) model through a crunchy, oversampled valve-amp model and a speaker-cabinet filter;
- the hi-hat and crash cymbals, as dense decaying noise that darkens as it rings, with faint inharmonic partials.

The reverb's impulse response is also generated by code. There are no samples, audio files or external instrument libraries.

Harmony, riffs and melodies are original. The crowd's stamp-stamp-clap is a generic body-percussion pattern, the kind Queen's "We Will Rock You" made famous, used here as a style reference; the music around it is our own. The offline render and balance scripts write only to `artifacts/`, which is not part of the repository.

## The reference recording (comparison only)

The optional "compare with the reference recording" switch plays **Ecstatic Vibrations, Totally Transcendent**, the recording Jovi chose as a benchmark for power. It is played through the visible [official Sea Power YouTube upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=knOXppaqBYY) and the [YouTube IFrame Player API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/iframe_api_reference).
- It does not drive the picture, is not aligned with it, and is never recorded.
- The recording was not downloaded, extracted or added to the repository.
- The [official Bandcamp track page](https://seapower.bandcamp.com/track/ecstatic-vibrations-totally-transcendent) has the release information. The project did not buy the track and has no licence to redistribute it.
- The original score copies neither its melody nor its arrangement.

Earlier approaches were removed in G2.3 and remain in the Git history:
- G2.2's YouTube-driven clock and local audio input;
- the baked synthesiser arrangements of G1–G2.1.

## Fonts and icons

The page uses system fonts. Shop signs are drawn by code, and icons are inline SVG. No fonts or textures are downloaded.
